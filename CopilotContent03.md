# Moonlit Poem 🌙

The night wraps softly, silver and slow,
A hush of light on the world below.
The moon climbs high with a gentle glow,
And whispers to dreams that drift below.

It shines on rooftops, quiet and bright,
On restless hearts in the velvet night.
A patient glow, calm as a prayer,
A lantern for thoughts we carry there.

It does not boast, it does not shout,
It simply glows and leads us out.
Through shadows deep and lonely streets,
It turns our fears to moonlit beats.

So when the world feels dark and cold,
Look up and let the moonlight hold.
A tender light, both soft and true,
Will guide the heart back home to you. 🌙

---

# Sitcom Scene: Moonlight and Its "Relevance" 🌙

[INT. SMALL TOWN APARTMENT - NIGHT]

A tiny apartment with one lamp, one couch, and the emotional weight of a lot of unpaid bills. The window is open. The moon is shining brightly like it has no idea how much rent is due.

MAYA, 28, tired but stylish in a way that says she gave up on being practical at 10 p.m., is pacing with a mug of tea.

JACK, 30, in a hoodie and sweatpants, is staring at the moon while pretending to be deep.

JACK
You know, the moon is kind of relevant.

MAYA
Relevant? To what? To my life? To my job? To the fact that I have to be at work in six hours and still haven't folded my laundry?

JACK
To the human experience.

MAYA
Oh, great. We are doing philosophy in a room with one lamp and a suspiciously moldy banana.

JACK
The moon has guided sailors, inspired art, affected tides, and shaped stories for centuries.

MAYA
And apparently now it is your life coach.

JACK
Listen, it's huge. It's in the sky. People write poems about it. People cry about it. It is literally a giant nighttime spotlight with no ego.

MAYA
No ego? The moon is glowing in the sky like it owns the neighborhood. That's ego.

JACK
Okay, but that's also beauty.

MAYA
Beauty is not a plan, Jack. Beauty is what happens when you think you're making a healthy choice and then you order fries at 11:30 p.m.

JACK
You don't understand the moon's importance.

MAYA
I understand it enough to know that if the moon disappeared, there would be no one to ask, “Why is it so dark in the hallway?”

JACK
That is not the point.

MAYA
No, that is the point. The moon is a mood. A giant mood. People look at it and suddenly they feel poetic, or dramatic, or like they should call an ex.

JACK
Exactly! That means it matters.

MAYA
It matters like a candle matters in a power outage. Nice to have. Not exactly civilization.

JACK
You are so cynical.

MAYA
I am a woman with a to-do list and a leaking ceiling. Cynicism is my backup personality.

JACK
You know what? Let's say the moon never existed. No moonlight. No tides. No nighttime romantic scenes. No dramatic speeches outside apartment windows.

MAYA
That sounds like a better world.

JACK
You would never say that if you were standing under it with someone you cared about.

MAYA
I would say it even harder. The moon is a terrible wingman.

JACK
A terrible wingman?

MAYA
Yes. It creates this false sense of romance. “Oh, look, the moon is shining.” Suddenly everybody is deep and emotional and saying things like, “We should spend more time together.” Meanwhile, I still can't find my charger.

JACK
You make everything sound transactional.

MAYA
Because life is transactional. There are taxes, there are bills, there is a man in the hallway who leaves his garbage by the elevator and pretends it's art.

JACK
You know what the moon does, though? It reminds us we're part of something bigger.

MAYA
Okay, that's actually kind of nice.

JACK
See? I told you it was relevant.

MAYA
I mean... yes. It is kind of relevant. In the same way an old song is relevant when you're feeling nostalgic and a little stupid.

JACK
That is the most poetic insult I've ever heard.

MAYA
Thank you. I work hard on being both helpful and devastating.

JACK
So the moon matters.

MAYA
The moon matters. But also, if we are being honest, the real reason it matters is because it looks beautiful and makes people stop talking for a second.

JACK
That counts.

MAYA
It counts. It is basically the world's most reliable mood ring.

JACK
So we're agreed.

MAYA
We're agreed that the moon is relevant, dramatic, and completely unnecessary for human survival.

JACK
Exactly.

MAYA
And yet, somehow, here we are, talking about it at 11:18 p.m.

JACK
Because the moon is a conversation starter.

MAYA
Or a distraction.

JACK
They're the same thing, Maya.

MAYA
No, they're different. One is art. The other is me pretending I don't have to do my laundry.

JACK
That is honestly the most moon-related thing you've ever said.

MAYA
Then maybe the moon is relevant after all.

JACK
Told you.

MAYA
Fine. The moon is relevant. But I still think the sun has the better publicist.

JACK
That is the most disrespectful thing you've ever said about the solar system.

MAYA
I said it with respect. I was being subtle.

JACK
Subtle? The moon is glowing above us and you act like it's a bad roommate.

MAYA
A bad roommate with an excellent lighting setup.

JACK
That is actually impressive.

MAYA
Thank you. I can make a whole career out of that kind of commentary.

They both look up at the moon. It's huge, gentle, and unbelievably smug.

JACK
So... are we going to be philosophical forever, or are we going to order pizza?

MAYA
The moon made us talk about meaning. Pizza is the answer to all existential crises.

JACK
That is the most practical thing you've said all night.

MAYA
And yet somehow I still feel like the moon won.

JACK
Of course it did.

MAYA
Great. Now go get the menu.

JACK
Yes, your highness.

MAYA
And if you call me that again, I am leaving you to the moon.

JACK
Not a chance. I need a witness to my deep thoughts.

MAYA
You have the moon.

JACK
Exactly.

They smile, as if the universe itself has made a joke at their expense.

CUT TO BLACK.

# End scene
